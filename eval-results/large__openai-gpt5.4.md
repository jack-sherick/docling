Across the three solicitations, the common regional theme is a strong need for **capacity-backed resources with credible deliverability**, rather than energy-only offers without transmission certainty. Avista is seeking a large near-term block of seasonal capacity—up to **415 MW winter** and **425 MW summer**—plus up to **200 aMW** of renewable/non-emitting energy and early demand response. Idaho Power is targeting at least **200 MW** for **2031–2032**, with a clear preference for either resource-backed capacity or firm WRAP-eligible market purchases. PSE’s excerpts do not provide a total MW target, but the structure of its voluntary utility-scale RFP shows interest in a broad set of CETA-compliant energy and capacity resources, including storage, renewables, and some fueled resources under specific conditions.

A significant regional constraint visible in the excerpts is **interconnection and transmission concentration risk**. Avista’s posted transmission summary shows especially tight apparent availability at some delivery points—most notably **Washington Mid-C at 0 MW**, very limited **Eastern Montana (8 MW)** and **Idaho (33 MW)**, and only moderate posted availability at **BPA (64 MW)**, while Montana appears more open. Avista is also aligning bids with its **2025 cluster study**, underscoring that queue timing and study progress will be important differentiators. Idaho Power likewise requires bidders to show active GI queue participation and include ICIF/TPIF costs, suggesting that speculative projects without mature interconnection positions may struggle. PSE’s west-of-Cascades and on-system delivery preference, plus storage charging requirements and external-resource transmission obligations, point to additional competition for deliverable transmission rights. Taken together, the area appears to be soliciting many of the same resource classes—renewables, storage, firm capacity products, and demand-side capability—while relying on constrained and highly process-dependent interconnection pathways, which could materially affect the ability to procure all needed resources on schedule.
