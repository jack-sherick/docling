Across the retrieved excerpts, the clearest regional theme is a broad push for **capacity-focused procurement with flexible technology eligibility**, but with strong emphasis on deliverability and interconnection readiness. Avista is seeking sizable seasonal capacity needs, up to **415 MW winter** and **425 MW summer**, plus up to **200 aMW** of renewable or non-emitting energy and early demand response. Idaho Power is seeking at least **200 MW** of incremental perfect capacity in 2031/2032 and is explicitly allowing both resource-based projects and WRAP-eligible market purchases. PSE’s excerpted materials do not state a quantitative need, but they clearly show a utility-scale procurement framework spanning renewables, storage, and structured acquisition products.

A significant common constraint across all three solicitations is **interconnection maturity and transmission deliverability**. Avista requires a detailed interconnection and delivery plan and evaluates on-system bids based on interconnection progress. Idaho Power is especially explicit that bids must show GI queue status, transmission rights or requests, and include ICIF/TPIF costs; resources must either be inside the IPC balancing area or show transmission rights into it. PSE similarly requires interconnection requests, transmission plans, and studies, and for storage adds the extra complexity of being studied as both generation and load where required. Taken together, these requirements suggest that the area’s ability to obtain needed resources may be limited less by technology eligibility than by **queue position, network upgrade exposure, deliverability into the relevant balancing areas, and the administrative burden of proving commercial readiness**. Storage projects may face particularly complex implementation because they often require both charging arrangements and generation interconnection treatment.
