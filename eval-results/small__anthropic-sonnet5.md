## Narrative Summary

Across the three solicitations, Avista and Idaho Power are both explicitly driven by IRP-identified capacity shortfalls and are seeking firm, dispatchable, and renewable capacity within a similar 2027–2032 timeframe, while PSE's RFP is framed as "voluntary" with no stated capacity target in the retrieved excerpts, suggesting its need may be more CETA-compliance-oriented (REC/attribute driven) rather than a hard capacity gap. Notably, both Avista and Idaho Power reference the Western Resource Adequacy Program (WRAP) for capacity qualification, indicating these two utilities are drawing from overlapping capacity accreditation frameworks and potentially the same regional generation and storage development pipeline in the Inland Northwest/Intermountain West. This overlap — combined with Avista's and Idaho Power's shared emphasis on wind, storage, and gas-fired or market-purchase capacity products with similar 2029–2032 online targets — could create competitive pressure on shared transmission corridors, interconnection queue slots, and developer/EPC resources if bidders are proposing similar project types in adjoining or shared balancing authority areas.

Interconnection requirements differ in specificity and risk allocation: Avista and Idaho Power place heavy emphasis on demonstrated progress in generator interconnection studies (cluster study participation for Avista; GI request status and ICIF/TPIF cost estimates for Idaho Power), signaling that both utilities are wary of "paper" projects without real queue standing — a reasonable precaution given that both areas likely face constrained transmission capacity and long interconnection study queues typical of the WECC footprint. PSE's requirements are similarly stringent on interconnection cost responsibility (fully seller-funded) and impose additional technical hurdles for storage (dual generation/load studies) and variable resources (minimum one-year performance data), but the excerpts do not indicate whether PSE's west-of-Cascades transmission corridor faces similar queue congestion as the Idaho Power/Avista footprint.

Because the excerpts do not specify PSE's total capacity need, COD target, or resource need drivers, and because Idaho Power's excerpts do not provide a stated minimum MW per resource, a full quantitative comparison of regional resource adequacy pressure is limited. However, the overlapping WRAP participation and shared emphasis on wind and storage procurement by Avista and Idaho Power suggest these two utilities are the most likely to compete directly for the same class of resources and interconnection capacity in the near term (2027–2032), while PSE's solicitation—being voluntary and CETA-driven—may draw a broader or different developer pool focused on variable renewable and storage assets primarily in western Washington.
